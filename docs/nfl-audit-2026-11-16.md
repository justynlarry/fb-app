## Summary

1. **No lookahead found in the walk-forward itself.** Ratings, the points model, QB values and the replacement
   level all use only earlier games. Line signs, neutral sites and missing lines all check out.
2. **We have no NFL line we could have bet.** The only line is nflverse's single number per game: no opener, no
   timestamp, no book name, and its source changes at least twice (2023 and 2025). Every NFL result so far is
   "accuracy against roughly the close". Nothing can be said about edge until we have openers.
3. **The 3+ point "Plays" on the NFL page are underdog picks, not model insight.** Our line is too flat, so 87% of
   3+ gaps are on the underdog. They went 50.9% ± 4.5 (n=483); betting every underdog blind went 51.0% ± 2.6.
4. **One live bug is coming in January:** ESPN numbers playoff weeks 1–5, nflverse 19–22, and both write the same
   `games.week` column. When ESPN's number is in place, the QB adjustment for playoff games ignores the whole
   current season.
5. **The QB adjustment's gain is real but it is "kickoff knowledge".** 93% of its accuracy gain comes from the 20%
   of games where a starter changed since the team's previous game, which is the news the market prices at once.
   Also, nflverse's "starter" is wrong in 54 team-games (33 in 2024).
6. **Verdict:** at the close there is no edge and I don't expect one (slope −0.06 ± 0.10). If the NFL has anything
   for us it is in timing and price, and price matters far more than in college: getting +3.5 instead of +3 is
   worth about 5 points of win rate, more than the whole college model edge.

### The model: `nfl-v2`
- **Ratings** (shared code, `cfb/ratings.py`): for each of seven per-game numbers (EPA/play, success rate,
  explosiveness, pass EPA, rush EPA, plays, points) a ridge regression gives every team an offense and a defense
  rating, adjusted for opponent and home field. Recent games count more (half-life 6 weeks). Last season's games
  count at half weight and fade out over 12 weeks.
- **Points model** (`cfb/nfl_model.py`): plain linear regression predicting one team's points from its 7 offense
  ratings, the opponent's 7 defense ratings, home field, and two QB terms. Line = away − home, total = sum.
  Each season is predicted by a model trained only on earlier seasons. 2020 feeds ratings but not training.
- **QB adjustment** (`cfb/nfl_qbs.py`): a QB's value is his career EPA per dropback above replacement level,
  shrunk toward replacement until he has a few hundred dropbacks (K = 410). The feature is the starter's value
  minus the value of the QBs who played the games the ratings were built on.
- **Live:** the worker reloads the season from nflverse and re-predicts at 6:30 AM Mountain. The NFL tab shows our
  line, the nflverse line and a Play/Pass tier.

## 2. Bugs and leakage, ranked by impact

### 2.1 No bettable line exists in our NFL data (evaluation)
**Evidence.**
- `lines.spread_open` is NULL for all 2,924 NFL rows. nflverse publishes one spread per game.
- The file gives no book and no time. Its character changes by season:

  | Seasons | Average vig on the spread prices | Totals on a half point | Spreads exactly on 3 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 2012–2022 | 2.0–2.7% | 38–53% | 11–16% |
  | 2023–2024 | 4.7% | 55–58% | 16–17% |
  | 2025–2026 | 4.7% | **100%** | 10% (2025), 19.5% (2026) |

  So it is a low-vig consensus through 2022, a retail-priced source from 2023, and a different one again from
  2025. Spread pushes follow: 4.9% of games in 2023, 0.4% in 2025.
- In 2026 it matches ESPN's scoreboard line: of 12 week-10 games where our poller saved ESPN's last pre-kickoff
  line, 11 match exactly. The twelfth (Carolina at New Orleans) has the **opposite sign** (nflverse: Saints −1.5;
  ESPN: Panthers −1.5). I can't tell which is right.
- For games not yet played, nflverse shows a current line and we overwrite it daily under the same provider
  name, so no history is kept. Week 11's lines were already in the file before week 10's Monday game.

**Impact.** Every NFL number we have is "against roughly the close". College's edge existed only at the open and
was zero at the close, so the NFL model has never been tested where it might work. The half-point habits of the
2025+ source also change which games push on 3.

**Fix.** Collect our own NFL snapshots from now on (The Odds API, about 2 credits per snapshot, which fits the
500/month budget). For history, see Test 1. Until then, label the line on the NFL page "nflverse line (source and
time unknown)".

### 2.2 Playoff week numbers: ESPN and nflverse overwrite each other (live bug, January)
**Evidence.**
- ESPN's scoreboard numbers playoff weeks 1 (wild card) to 5 (Super Bowl). Checked today: Jan 10 2026 games return
  `week.number = 1`, the Super Bowl `5`.
- nflverse numbers them 19–22, and the database currently holds 19–22 for 2025.
- `espn.sync_schedule` runs on every poll and sets `week = EXCLUDED.week`. `nflverse.load_games` sets it back at
  6:30 AM. The two run in different threads, so either can be in place when `predict_upcoming` builds its rows.

**Impact.** `nfl_qbs.py` orders games by `season * 100 + week`. With week = 1, a playoff game sorts before the whole
regular season. `value()` then sees none of the QB's current-season dropbacks, and `team_recent()` finds no games
and returns 0, so `qb_adj` becomes the starter's old career value with nothing subtracted. At about 26 points per
1.0 EPA/dropback that is +3 to +5 points for each good QB (my arithmetic from the code; not run). In the spread the two sides partly cancel; the total
would be several points too high. It affects all 13 playoff predictions, the games people care most about.

**Fix.** One writer for `week`. Simplest: in `sync_schedule`, convert ESPN's postseason week to nflverse's
(18 + ESPN week, Super Bowl = 22) before saving. A small Python change for you to type; I'll give it in chunks.

### 2.3 The Play tier is produced by the flat line (display / interpretation)
**Evidence (nfl-v2, 2021–25).**
- Our spreads have SD 4.8, the market's 6.1. A flat line disagrees with the market on every big favorite, always
  in the underdog's direction.
- 87% of 3+ gaps are on the underdog (95% for nfl-v1).
- Those picks: 50.9% ± 4.5 (n=483). Every underdog, no model: 51.0% ± 2.6 (n=1,391). Underdogs of 7+: 52.2% ± 5.0.

**Impact.** A "Play" on the NFL page mostly means "the favorite is big". The page already says the tiers are not
proven; this is the reason. Fixing the flatness (T-MATCH did: SD 6.0, calibration 1.00 in-sample) is a
precondition for reading any NFL gap at all.

**Fix.** Until the line is calibrated and graded at a bettable line, drop the Play styling on the NFL tab (template
change, mine to make if you agree).

### 2.4 The backtest knows the actual starter; at bet time we often wouldn't
**Evidence.** nfl-v1 → nfl-v2, same games, 2021–25:

| Games | n | Avg change in our line | Miss v1 → v2 | Close | Share of the total gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A starter differs from the team's previous game (in season) | 286 | 2.8 pts | 10.66 → 10.29 | 9.95 | 93% |
| First game of a season with a new starter | 52 | 2.9 pts | 8.63 → 9.00 (worse) | 9.11 | −17% |
| Same starters as last game | 1,086 | 1.3 pts | 10.18 → 10.16 | 9.74 | 24% |

**Impact.** This was stated when T-QB was registered ("a small optimism"). It is not small in where it sits: nearly
all of the gain is in games where the starter is news. At the close that is fair, since the market knows too. At
an opener it is lookahead: many of those changes are announced Wednesday to Sunday. **Any future grading "at the
open" must use the starter known at that time**, or it will look better than it was.
The season-opener row (n=52, noise-level) hints that valuing rookies and new arrivals at replacement level, or at a
value earned in another offense, hurts in week 1.

**Fix.** Define a bet-time starter rule before any opener test (Test 1 has one).

### 2.5 nflverse lists the wrong starter in 54 team-games
**Evidence.** 54 completed team-games where the listed starter has no dropback at all: 4 in 2022, 33 in 2024, 7 in
2025, 10 already in 2026. Examples: Eagles week 17 2024 lists Jalen Hurts (Kenny Pickett played); Dolphins week 18
2024 lists Tua Tagovailoa (Tyler Huntley played); Jets weeks 10–17 2025 list Tyrod Taylor. These look like
projected starters that were never corrected. Separately, in 2.5% of team-games the listed starter isn't the QB
with the most dropbacks, which includes real in-game injuries.

**Impact.** No lookahead; the opposite. In about 6% of 2024 team-games the feature is wrong in exactly the games it
exists for. T-QB's gain is probably understated a little, and the training rows carry the errors.

**Fix.** For completed games, take the starter from the play-by-play (the QB with the team's first dropback).
Keep nflverse's name only for games not yet played.

### 2.6 QB careers start in 2016, so the feature's scale drifts
**Evidence.** The shrink factor n/(n + 410) of the average starter: 0.43 in 2017, 0.55 in 2019, 0.60 in 2021, 0.67
in 2025, 0.75 in 2026. Median prior dropbacks of a starter: 484 in 2017, 1,836 in 2025.

**Impact.** A veteran in 2017 is valued on one season; the same quality of QB in 2025 on eight. The points model
learns one weight for a feature whose spread grows every year. Honest (no lookahead) but it wastes information, and
career totals never fade, so a QB's 2016 season counts as much as last month.

**Fix.** Load pre-2016 play-by-play for QB careers (free), and consider fading old dropbacks. A model change, so it
is Test 4, not a fix.

### 2.7 Small items
- **Manual starters get overwritten.** `save_starters` sets `source = 'nflverse'` on conflict, so a hand-entered
  starter is replaced as soon as nflverse lists one. No entry form exists yet; fix before building it.
- **Predictions beyond next week have no QB adjustment** (no projected starter yet). Fine, but the page shows them
  without saying so.
- **Penalty plays are in the stats.** 4.2% of kept plays are `no_play` penalties with average EPA +0.19 and larger
  swings than normal plays. This is the usual nflverse convention. Not a bug; a possible noise source.
- **Garbage-time filter is heavy in blowouts.** It drops 14.7% of plays; 5.1% of team-games keep under 30 plays
  (minimum 8). Ratings weight by plays kept, so a blowout counts less, while the `points` metric still uses the
  full-game score.
- **All playoff games count as "this week"** in the recency weights. Negligible.
- **Replacement level for 2016** uses 2016 itself (stated). Negligible.
- **K = 410** came from reliability measured on 2016–25, which includes the judging seasons. Negligible.
- **± band** is the average miss, not an interval (same as college audit 1.5).
- **Moneyline vs spread** disagree on the favorite in 1–3 games a season (near pick'em). Source noise.

### Checked and found correct
- Stored lines equal −`spread_line` in 2,924 of 2,924 rows. Home/away and the sign flip are right.
- Neutral flags match the file in all 3,033 games, and ESPN agrees (Madrid 2025 checked). International games and
  Super Bowls get home = 0 in ratings and in the points model.
- No missing spread or total, 2016–2026. Pushes are excluded from win rates in the view.
- The loader reads `wp` (no point spread inside), never `vegas_wp` or the play-by-play's `spread_line`. No market
  number reaches the model.
- Week W ratings use games that kicked off before week W's first game. The points model for season S is trained on
  seasons before S. QB values use earlier weeks only; replacement level is the previous season's.
- 2020 is out of training and out of every evaluation.
- **Stats by season are steady** (no college-style break): plays per team-game 53.9–55.9, EPA/play −0.02 to +0.04,
  success rate 0.42–0.46, dropbacks per team-game 31.2–33.0.
- A backtest run can't overwrite live predictions (`save_predictions` guard).

## 3. Method findings

### How much each team stat repeats (regular season 2016–25, no lines)
"Split-half" = a team's odd games vs its even games in one season. 1.0 = perfectly repeatable, 0 = noise.

| Stat | Offense split-half | Offense year to year | Defense split-half | Defense year to year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Success rate | 0.59 | 0.41 | 0.37 | 0.26 |
| Points | 0.57 | 0.37 | 0.36 | 0.28 |
| EPA/play | 0.55 | 0.38 | 0.37 | 0.28 |
| Pass EPA | 0.49 | 0.34 | 0.33 | 0.21 |
| Rush EPA | 0.29 | 0.34 | 0.25 | 0.23 |
| Explosiveness | 0.24 | 0.10 | 0.16 | 0.26 |
| Plays | 0.08 | 0.12 | 0.05 | 0.15 |

Weeks 1–9 stat vs weeks 10+ scoring margin (320 team-seasons): offense EPA 0.42, success rate 0.43, pass EPA 0.41,
rush EPA 0.27; defense EPA −0.30, defense success rate −0.19.

What that says:
- **Offense is about twice as repeatable as defense.** A half-season defense rating is mostly noise.
- **Passing predicts, rushing barely does.** Plays (pace) and explosiveness are close to noise.
- **Our ratings shrink every stat by the same small amount** (ridge alpha 1, about one game's worth). These numbers
  imply about 7 games' worth for offense EPA and about 14 for defense. The points model partly makes up for it by
  learning small weights, which is one reason the line is flat, and one weight can't suit both week 3 and week 14
  (model-tests.md: too steep early, too flat late).

### Keep
- **Walk-forward structure, 2020 handling, per-team points rows** as the baseline every test is compared with.
- **Slope plus MAE at the close** as the accuracy measures. Game-level bootstrap is adequate (matches by-week).
- **One home-field coefficient.** Home teams won by 2.25 ± 0.75 a game in 2021–25 (2012–18: 2.58 ± 0.66). The market's
  implied home edge fell from 2.4 to 1.5–1.8, and home teams beat the close by 0.6–1.1 in 2023–25, but they covered
  only 49.8–52.0%. Nothing to bet; nothing to change.
- **No rest features.** Teams with 3+ more days of rest covered 48.3% ± 6.0 (home, n=265) and 53.1% ± 5.6 (away,
  n=303); teams off a bye 47.5% and 50.8% (n=120 each). Priced in, as in college.

### Change
- **Stop judging accuracy by "better in 4 of 5 seasons".** One season's paired MAE difference has an SE of about
  0.10, so a real 0.05 gain wins a given season about 69% of the time and passes 4 of 5 only about half the time.
  Use the pooled paired difference with a bootstrap SE and report seasons as information. T-MATCH's 0.053 gain was
  about 1.2 paired SE either way, so this doesn't reverse it.
- **Calibration is a goal in itself.** With calibration 1.14 the line can't be compared with the market
  (section 2.3). Judge a calibration fix by the calibration slope, with MAE as a no-harm check.
- **More seasons.** Five judging seasons give a slope SE of 0.10. nflverse has play-by-play and the same line file
  back to 1999. Sixteen-plus seasons would cut every SE by almost half, and 2006–2016 have never been looked at, so
  they are clean for ideas found on 2021–25 (Test 2).

### Test
- **Full matchup rows / calibration** (T-MATCH again, unchanged, on clean seasons): Test 3.
- **QB value from full careers** with fading and a rookie prior: Test 4.
- **Proper shrinkage toward a prior** (each rating shrunk by its own reliability, toward last season's regressed
  rating, weights growing with games played). This is the principled version of T-WEEK and of "ratings that evolve
  week to week". Worth doing after Test 3, on clean seasons, because both attack the same flatness. Not in this
  plan's five.

### Key numbers and P(cover)
- Final margins 2016–25: 3 = 14.7%, 7 = 8.5%, 6 = 6.7%, 14 = 5.1%, 10 = 4.7% (college: 3 = 9.4%, 7 = 7.9%).
- When the line is exactly 3, the favorite wins by exactly 3 in 9.7% of games (n=390). On exactly 7: 5.2% (n=153).
- **So +3.5 instead of +3 turns 9.7% of results from pushes into wins: about +4.9 points of win rate.** −2.5
  instead of −3 is worth about the same. Around 7 it is about +2.6. A point elsewhere is worth about 2.5–3.
- P(cover) from our own margin: SD of (result − close) is 12.7 for spreads and 13.2 for totals. But a naive
  "normal around our line" would repeat college's mistake: with a slope of zero at the close, a 5-point gap is
  worth 0 points, not 5. The right formula is P = 50% + (slope × gap) × (chance per point near that line, using the
  real key-number frequencies above) and it only becomes useful once some line shows a positive slope. Until then
  the key numbers matter for **which price to take**, not for which side.

### Season phase (hypothesis from college; hindsight cut here)
nfl-v2 at the close, 2021–25: weeks-played 0–4 slope −0.08 ± 0.21, ATS 3+ 54.6% ± 8.9 (n=119); 5–9 slope −0.04,
51.4% ± 9.3 (n=111); 10+ slope −0.09 ± 0.14, 47.8% ± 6.5 (n=228). No pattern, and nothing like college's. It can't
be checked at the open.

## 5. Other angles

- **Openers and lookahead lines.** The NFL posts lookahead lines a week ahead (and in May for every game) and
  re-opens on Sunday evening. This is the same trap as DraftKings in college, and also the only place college
  showed anything. The Odds API's history (featured markets from June 2020, snapshots every 5–10 minutes) would
  give true timed lines from many books for 2020–25. It is a **paid** call: at the reported 10 credits per market
  per region, about 800 snapshots × 20 credits ≈ 16,000 credits, which is one month of the $30 plan. I have not
  verified the price on their site. Tests 1 and 5 use it.
- **Totals.** Slope −0.04 ± 0.13 at the close; we lean over (65% of 3+ gaps). Nothing there.
- **Wind and totals (hypothesis only, hindsight, observed wind not forecast).** Outdoor games 2016–25: wind 10–19
  mph went under 58.4% ± 4.1 (n=560); under 10 mph 47.7% ± 2.9. In 1999–2015, 10–14 mph was 51.4% but 15+ was
  57.4% (n=472). College Test 7 rejected the same idea with forecasts. Worth logging NFL forecasts next to the
  college ones; not worth a test now. Wind is missing for 9% of outdoor games.
- **Game types.** Playoffs n=65, neutral sites n=32: too few to say anything.
- **Price and timing across books.** We track no NFL books. This is the cheapest thing to start: the existing
  snapshot job with the NFL added.

### Acceptance rules, written now
Shared details: paired bootstrap of games, 2,000 resamples, both models on the same resamples. 2020 excluded.
"Close" = the nflverse line. No variants after results.

**1. T-OPEN.**
- *Bet-time line:* the median spread across books (4+ books) in the snapshot nearest Monday 9:00 AM Eastern of game
  week, which is when our morning job has Sunday's games. Reported beside it: Sunday 9:00 PM Eastern.

### Count and false-pass risk
Five model runs. Four are judged (1, 3, 4, 5), each at 2.24 SE (one-sided 1.25%): the chance that at least one
passes by luck is about 5%. NFL models tried so far: the 36-setting grid (chosen without lines) and five tests;
this plan makes ten.

### Not proposed
- Anything already rejected (T-skill, T-CAL, T-WEEK).
- A lineup-weighted rating from snap shares: large build, and only QBs have a value that repeats.
- Offensive-line cluster injuries, wind for totals, late-season stakes: hypotheses to log, each needing its own
  registration later. Late-season stakes is already in the parking lot.
- Rest, travel, division games, home-field changes: priced in or already parked.

## 7. Verdict

**How much is realistic.** At the close: nothing. The slope is −0.06 ± 0.10, the market beats us by 0.38 points of
average miss, and it is a sharper market than college with far fewer games. At an opener: unknown, because we have
never had one. College's whole edge was anticipating about half a point of line movement, worth roughly break-even.
NFL openers are sharper and move less, so my expectation is 50–52% against a 52.4% break-even, with a 95% range
over one season of about ±10 on roughly 100 picks.

**What it would take to know.** About 100 picks of 3+ a season:
- One season's win rate: ±10. Five seasons: ±4.5.
- Telling 55% from 52.4% with 80% power takes about 2,300 picks, about 23 seasons.
- A college-sized slope of +0.10 needs an SE of 0.045 to clear 2.24; that is about 26 seasons of games.
- Line movement toward our picks is the only signal that can show up in two or three seasons.

**Where edge is most likely, in order.**
1. **Price.** A half point through 3 is worth about 5 points of win rate, and through 7 about 2.6. Shopping nine
   books for the hook, or for −105 instead of −110, pays without the model being right more often. This needs NFL
   snapshots, which we don't collect yet.
2. **Timing.** Openers and lookahead lines, if Tests 1 and 5 show the line moving toward us.
3. **News.** Injuries and QB changes are where the market's 0.4-point lead comes from. We will not be faster than
   it; overrides can only stop bad bets.
4. **Player data.** A better QB rating should narrow the gap to the close by a few hundredths. Other positions:
   override sizes only.
5. **The model.** Its job in the NFL is an honest, calibrated independent line to hang price and timing decisions
   on. Right now it is neither calibrated nor graded at a line we could bet.

**First steps I'd suggest, in order (all waiting for your go-ahead):** fix the playoff week numbering before
January (2.2); start NFL odds snapshots; decide on the history purchase for Test 1; run Test 2 in scratch.
