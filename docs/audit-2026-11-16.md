# Independent audit, 2026-11-16 (Stage 1: nothing fixed, no tests run)

Scope: CLAUDE.md, docs/model-tests.md, reference/, parking-lot.md, `cfb/` (model, ratings, priors, picks,
coaches, odds, worker, load), `db/init/`, and the scratch research scripts (`t6.py`, `coach_adj.py`).
Every number below comes from read-only queries on the live database. **0 CFBD calls, no model was refit,
no file other than this one was changed.** "Bovada open" means the stored v5 backtest predictions re-graded
against one book's opening line instead of the average of books.

## Summary

1. **The headline survives.** I reproduced 1,134–1,030 (52.4%) at the open exactly. Walk-forward code has no
   lookahead that I can find.
2. **One real evaluation problem:** CFBD's `spreadOpen` is not the same thing for every book. Bovada's is a true
   Sunday-evening opener. DraftKings' is a lookahead line posted days (sometimes months) before the previous
   week's games. Our model uses those games, so against DraftKings' "open" it looks far better than it is
   (55.4%). By luck the averaged headline is almost unaffected (Bovada alone: 52.39%), but "best open 53.4%",
   2026's "at open" figures, the Caution tier and holdout 5A all lean on the mixed number.
3. **All of the edge at the open is in the second half of the season.** FBS-vs-FBS games at the Bovada open:
   weeks-played 0–4 went 47.6% (n=693, slope ≈ 0); weeks-played 5+ went 54.1% (n=1,226, slope +0.22 ± 0.07).
   This is a hindsight cut, and 2026 by month does not obviously repeat it, so it's a hypothesis, not a rule.
4. **Verdict:** as a whole-season system at −110 this is break-even. The model's edge is roughly the line move it
   anticipates (about 0.56 pts on a 6-pt gap). What's left is most likely in timing and price (Sunday openers,
   best number across nine books) and in news via overrides, not in another model feature.

## 1. Bugs and leakage, ranked by likely impact

### 1.1 "Opening line" mixes true openers with stale lookahead lines (evaluation, not model)
**What.** `backtest_games` and `UPCOMING_SQL` use `avg(spread_open)` over all books. Opens exist for Bovada
(2021+), DraftKings (2023+) and ESPN Bet (2024–25).

**Evidence.**
- Week 12 of 2026, CFBD open vs our own timed Odds API snapshots, same book:

  | Book | Snapshot that matches CFBD's "open" | Exact matches | Avg difference from Sunday 8 PM line |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Bovada | Sunday Nov 15, 8 PM MT | 56 of 65 | 0.07 |
  | DraftKings | Thursday Nov 12 (before week 11 was played) | 8 of 10 | 1.46 |

- CFBD already lists DraftKings "opens" for 14 week-13 games and for Army–Navy (Dec 12).
- The two books' openers differ by 2+ points in 18 of 66 week-12 games.
- How much the line moves per point that a team beat the spread *last week*: Bovada −0.012, ESPN Bet −0.015,
  DraftKings −0.032. DraftKings' "open" is reacting to games that had not been played when it was posted.
- v5 re-graded per book, 2023–25:

  | Open used | n | Slope at open | ATS 3+ | CLV pts |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Bovada | 2,557 | +0.101 | 52.4% (n=1,266) | 0.68 |
  | DraftKings | 2,150 | +0.225 | 55.4% (n=1,123) | 1.35 |
  | ESPN Bet | 1,696 | +0.043 | 53.6% (n=832) | 0.85 |

**Impact.**
- Headline 2021–25: mixed 52.40% (n=2,164) vs Bovada-only 52.39% (1,097–997, n=2,094, ±2.1). Slope +0.104 vs
  +0.113 ± 0.048. CLV points 0.61 vs 0.56. So the main numbers hold.
- "Best open 53.4%" picks the more favourable of the books' opens, which from 2023 on is often the stale one.
  It should be dropped, not just labelled an upper bound.
- 2026 has only Bovada and DraftKings opens, so every 2026 "at open" number is half lookahead. That includes
  the game set and line for **holdout 5A**.
- Live: the Caution tier ("moved against us since the open") compares the current line with this mixed open.

**Fix.** Define the open as one book (Bovada) everywhere, with that same book's close for CLV. In the view
that is `avg(spread_open) FILTER (WHERE provider = 'Bovada')` (SQL for you to type; I'll give it in chunks if
you approve). For 2027, use our own snapshots: the first snapshot on or after Sunday 4 PM MT of game week.
Amend 5A and pre-registration C to this definition **before** Dec 7.

### 1.2 The "first pick" in `pick_results` can be a lookahead pick made with last week's ratings
**What.** `first_pick` is the earliest `pick_log` row before kickoff. The Odds API returns lookahead lines, so
week-12 games were first recorded on Thursday Nov 12 (10–15 games, 2–3 Plays), when our line for them was still
1.8–2.6 pts away from the line we had after Saturday's games were in.

**Impact.** Small so far, but pre-registration C grades "the first pick before kickoff" as the early bet. It
would mix two different bets: a Thursday lookahead bet without last week's games, and a Sunday opener bet.

**Fix.** Grade the first pick recorded on or after Sunday of game week (or after both teams' previous games are
final). Keep the lookahead picks, tagged separately.

### 1.3 Tier rates on the predictions page are measured at the close, but the tiers are used early in the week
**What.** `make_pick()` and `PICK_RATES_SQL` apply the same rule (checked line by line; they match, apart from
`make_pick` calling a game with no open a Play). But the backtest applies it to the closing line, and live
applies it to whatever the median is on Sunday–Friday.

**Evidence.** Backtest tiers 2021–25: Play 51.7% (n=1,134), Caution 49.5% (n=979), Pass 50.7% (n=2,115).
Play vs Caution is 2.1 points apart with an SE of about 2.2, so the split is not established, and Play is below
break-even. The rule also "looked at 2026" (model-tests.md).

**Fix.** Either show the rate for the line the pick is actually made against (Bovada open, 52.4%), or label
the tier rates "graded at the close". No model change.

### 1.4 The stats feed changes in 2022, and metric levels drift by season
**What.** `/stats/game/advanced` covers about 870–890 games a season through 2021 (FBS-involved only) and
1,450–1,650 from 2022 (FCS-vs-FCS games included). `GAME_STATS_SQL` takes them all, so from 2022 FCS teams are
rated on full seasons and before that only on their FBS games. Separately, league-average pass EPA per play is
0.15–0.18 in 2016–19, 0.23 in 2021–23, 0.19–0.20 in 2024–26; overall EPA/play is 0.153 in 2021 vs about 0.11
in other years. Plays per team-game fall from 64.8 to about 60.

**Impact.** Unknown, probably small on spreads (errors in FBS-vs-FCS games show no clear break at 2022). It is
a plausible contributor to the totals bias: the points model assumes "0.20 pass EPA" means the same thing every
season. Not measured; I did not refit anything.

**Fix.** A model change, so it needs a test (see 3, Test D).

### 1.5 The ± band is the average miss, not an interval, and it ignores FCS
The band is the backtest MAE for the weeks-played bucket. 56–61% of games land inside it. FBS-vs-FCS games in
weeks 0–1 miss by 13.9–14.0 against a band of 13.3–13.4. Fix: label it "typical miss", or show an 80% band
(about 1.6× wider), and bucket FCS games separately. Display only.

### 1.6 Small items
- **CLV %** skips games where the line didn't move, and compares an open from 1–3 books with a close from up
  to 5. Same-book grading (1.1) fixes both.
- **Averaged lines** such as −6.75 can't push and can't be bet. Negligible; fixed by 1.1.
- **`/talent`** for every season was fetched on 2026-11-10. The composite is finalised after the season
  starts, so weeks 0–3 may see slightly better talent numbers than were available. Can't be checked without
  archived copies; early season shows no edge anyway, so it isn't inflating anything.
- **`teams.classification`** is today's value, so teams that moved up later count as FBS when priors are
  standardised for earlier seasons. Negligible.
- **Worker:** 26 ESPN poll failures on Nov 15 (19:39–19:45 UTC) with "self-signed certificate", plus one DNS
  failure and 13 weather timeouts. None in the last 3 hours. Something on the network briefly intercepted TLS;
  worth a look at the router or DNS filter if it recurs.

### Checked and found correct
- Week W never sees a week-W game: ratings use `start_time <` the week's first kickoff.
- The points model for season S is trained only on seasons before S; features in training rows are themselves
  walk-forward. No scaler or ridge is fitted on future rows.
- Coach-change dates come from end-of-season data but describe facts public at the time; the feature switches
  on only for later games. Coach-career residuals (`t6.py`) use seasons before S only.
- Sign conventions agree across `lines`, `predictions`, the views, the Odds API swap for neutral sites and the
  nflverse flip. Result regressed on our line has slope 1.01 (market 1.01).
- Neutral sites get home = 0 in both the ratings and the points model.
- Sunday's 5 AM run already has Saturday's stats: our Sunday lines differ from Monday's by 0.10 on average.
- The two teams' point errors are uncorrelated (r = 0.02), so predicting each side separately costs nothing.

## 2. Method findings

### Keep
- **Slope as the main metric (Rule B).** It uses every game and pairs the two models. Keep ATS 3+ as a
  supporting number.
- **Flat stakes and a fixed 3-point threshold.** At the Bovada open, ATS by gap size is 53.6% (3–5), 49.8%
  (5–7), 53.4% (7–10), 52.0% (10+). CLV grows with the gap (0.36 → 1.03 pts) but the win rate doesn't, so a
  P(cover) rule that bets more on bigger gaps isn't supported. "5+ = check news" is right.
- **Two rows per game.** Error correlation is 0.02.
- **Betting early.** Plays for week 12 went 18 → 22 → 13 between Sunday 4 PM, 8 PM and Monday 6 AM. The gap
  closes overnight.

### Change
- **One definition of "open"** (1.1), used by Rule B, the backtest page, 5A and C.
- **What the slope is worth, in plain terms.** A slope of 0.10 means a 6-point disagreement lands 0.6 pts our
  way, and each point is worth about 2.7% of win rate. Break-even at −110 needs about 0.9 pts, i.e. slope × gap
  ≥ 0.9. At 0.10 that never happens for normal gaps; at 0.22 (late season) a 4-point gap is enough. So judge
  changes by whether they move slope × gap toward 0.9, not by whether Δslope is "significant".
- **Multiple testing.** About 37 variants at a one-sided 5% bar means roughly 2 false passes are expected.
  T6-A at 2.1 SE is what chance alone would produce. Proposal: a backtest pass makes a *candidate* only; a
  budget of at most 4 new models before the 2027 season, each at 2.24 SE (one-sided 1.25%), so the chance that
  any passes by luck stays near 5%; adoption only after a frozen holdout.
- **Bootstrap by week, not by game,** and also report the slope with gaps clipped at ±10. A handful of
  15-point gaps (FCS, week 1) carry a lot of leverage in a plain regression.

### Test (ideas, in order of how much I believe them)
- **Season phase.** See Summary 3. The model's ratings need about five weeks of data before they tell the
  opener anything. Either don't bet before then, or fix the early-season model.
- **Same shrinkage for every metric.** `ALPHA = 1` shrinks a 10-game rating by 9% whether the metric is
  success rate (stable) or explosiveness (noisy). The points model can only compensate with one coefficient for
  the whole season, although a week-3 rating is far noisier than a week-10 one. Setting each metric's alpha
  from its own noise would make ratings equally trustworthy in every week. This is new: v4 tuned one alpha for
  all metrics.
- **Shrinking toward a prior instead of the league average / a state-space rating.** Statistically cleaner
  than hand-set fades. But Test 2's result and Round 3 (SP+) say better priors make us more accurate and more
  like the market. I'd expect accuracy in weeks 0–4, not edge, so it ranks low. Test 3 stays on the don't-retry
  list; I have no new information for it.
- **Objective.** Fitting points by least squares treats a 63–0 FCS game like a 24–21 conference game. Fitting
  margin directly on FBS-vs-FBS games is the obvious alternative. Low priority: the calibration slope is
  already 1.01.

**Acceptance rules, written now.**
- **A:** as written in model-tests.md 5A, with "opening line" = Bovada `spread_open`. Outcomes unchanged.
- **B:** no model. On 2026 games through Dec 6 at the Bovada open: *supported* if slope(weeks-played ≥ 5) −
  slope(0–4) > 0 **and** ATS 3+ for ≥ 5 is above ATS 3+ for 0–4. One season can't confirm it (about ±7 per
  group); 2026 by month has been seen at the mixed open (Sept 52.6%, Oct 52.6%), which already leans against.
  If not supported, drop it. If supported, apply it in 2027 and let pre-registration C judge.
- **C, D, E:** pass if Δslope ≥ 2.24 paired SE **and** ATS 3+ not down > 1.0 pp, CLV points not down > 2
  paired SE, spread MAE not worse. For D, total MAE must also improve. No variants after results. For C, the
  alphas are fixed from 2017–19 before any 2021–25 number is seen.

**Count and false-pass risk.** 4 new models (A, C, D, E). At 2.24 SE each, the chance at least one passes by
luck is about 5% (at today's 1.645 it would be 19%). Adding these makes about 41 variants in total.

**Not proposed:** anything on the don't-retry list; a state-space rewrite (large effort, expected to help
accuracy rather than edge); P(cover) staking (the gap table above argues against it).

| Source | What it has | Cost | Status |

| sportsdataverse play-by-play | Passer, rusher, receiver on every play with EPA and win probability, 2016–26 | 0 (files already downloaded in scratch) | Used once, for fumbles |
| ESPN | Player box scores in game summaries; no injuries | Free | Not used |
| nflverse | Player stats, snap counts, injuries, depth charts | Free | NFL only |
| The Odds API player props | Market prices | Credits | A market input: not allowed in the model |

**Would it make the model more accurate?** A little, early in the season. **Would it beat the opener?**
Probably not. Round 1 is the guide: who plays quarterback is the most closely priced information in the sport,
and our ratings absorb a change within a game. The two honest uses are:
1. **Overrides**: `/ppa/players/season` + `/player/usage` (2 calls per refresh) to size "player X is out" for
   non-QBs the way `qbs.py` does for QBs. This is aimed at the 1-point gap to the close, which is news.
2. **Test E**: a QB's own record carried into a new season or a new school. That is different from Round 1
   (in-season changes) and Round 3 (portal star ratings), which is why I'm willing to list it, last.

## 5. Verdict

- **Model.** v5 knows something the Sunday opener doesn't: slope +0.11 ± 0.05 at the Bovada open, positive in
  4 of 5 seasons (2025 was −0.05). It is worth about 0.6 points per pick, which is 52.4% and break-even. At the
  close the slope is zero. Thirty-seven variants have moved it by hundredths. I don't expect a feature to find
  another 2 points of win rate in this data.
- **Timing.** The edge exists for hours, at one low-limit book. Plays fell from 22 to 13 overnight on Sunday.
  More Sunday snapshots (credits allow it: about 94 of 500 used) would show how long the number lasts.
- **Price.** Best number and best price across nine books is the only edge that doesn't need the model to be
  right more often. `pick_log` already records it; it has four days of data.
- **Season phase.** If the weeks 5+ pattern is real, the same model is a 54% system for two-thirds of the
  season and a losing one for the rest. This is the single finding most worth confirming.
- **News.** The close beats us by a point of MAE, and gaps left at kickoff lost 44% this year. Overrides can
  stop bad bets; they won't create good ones against a market that has the same news.

Realistic expectation for 2027, betting Sunday openers at the best available number from week 6 on: somewhere
between 52% and 54%, with a 95% range over one season of about ±6. One season will not tell you which.
